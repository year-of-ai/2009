## Summary

The 2009 World Series ran from October 28 to November 4, 2009, with the New York Yankees defeating the defending-champion Philadelphia Phillies four games to two. The deciding Game 6 was played at the new Yankee Stadium in the Bronx, New York — the first World Series game ever played at the $1.5 billion ballpark, which had opened in April 2009 to replace the original stadium across the street. The Yankees won Game 6 by a score of 7–3.

Designated hitter Hideki Matsui was named World Series Most Valuable Player. In Game 6 alone, Matsui went 3-for-4 with a two-run home run and drove in six of the Yankees' seven runs. For the series, Matsui batted .615 (8-for-13) with 3 home runs and 8 runs batted in. Matsui became the first Japanese-born player to win World Series MVP honors, capping a distinguished career that had begun in Nippon Professional Baseball with the Yomiuri Giants.

## Key Players and Performances

The Yankees had rebuilt their roster aggressively in the 2008–09 offseason, signing starting pitcher CC Sabathia to a seven-year, $161 million contract (the richest pitching contract in history at the time), right-hander A.J. Burnett to a five-year deal, and first baseman Mark Teixeira to an eight-year, $180 million commitment. The three arrivals pushed New York's payroll to approximately $208 million, the highest in Major League Baseball by a significant margin.

Sabathia started Games 1 and 4 and was lined up for a potential Game 7; although Cliff Lee outdueled him in the opener — a 6–1 Phillies win — he anchored a rotation that also leaned on veteran left-hander Andy Pettitte, who won both Game 3 and the clinching Game 6. Alex Rodriguez, who had entered the postseason under scrutiny after his February 2009 admission to using performance-enhancing drugs from 2001 to 2003, delivered one of his finest playoff performances, batting .365 with 6 RBI across the series. Rodriguez's clutch hitting, after years of postseason struggles, was widely noted by commentators.

Manager Joe Girardi, in his second season, navigated the roster's veteran depth through a demanding October schedule and was credited with aggressive bullpen deployment that kept the Phillies' lineup — led by Ryan Howard and Jimmy Rollins — in check in the critical late innings.

## Significance

The 2009 championship was the Yankees' 27th World Series title, extending their record as the most successful franchise in professional baseball. The nine-year gap since their 2000 championship (which had also defeated the Mets in the "Subway Series") was unusually long for a club of New York's resources and had been a source of pointed criticism from fans and media.

The series also concluded a remarkable year of change for the Yankees organization. Playing their final season at the old Yankee Stadium in 2008 had been framed as an emotional farewell to a monument of American sports; winning the first World Series in the new stadium provided an immediate validation of the $1.5 billion investment.

The championship highlighted baseball's widening economic disparity: with a payroll more than three times that of smaller-market clubs, the Yankees demonstrated how sustained financial dominance could translate into roster depth sufficient to absorb injuries and maintain championship-caliber production.

## Broader Context

The 2009 World Series was the final championship event in a year packed with major American sports milestones. In February, the Pittsburgh Steelers had won the [Super Bowl XLIII](/news/people/super-bowl-xliii-2009/) in one of the most dramatic finishes in NFL history, and the Los Angeles Lakers had taken the [2009 NBA Finals](/news/people/nba-finals-2009/) over the Orlando Magic. The three championships together reflected a broader theme of storied franchises reclaiming elite status — Pittsburgh, Los Angeles, and New York each ending notable dry spells or defending legacies under scrutiny.

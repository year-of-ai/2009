## Summary

*Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen*, directed by Michael Bay and released June 24, 2009 in North America (after a June 8 Tokyo world premiere), was the sequel to 2007's *Transformers*. Set two years after the first film, the narrative follows Sam Witwicky (Shia LaBeouf) as he enrolls in college and becomes entangled in escalating conflict between Autobots and Decepticons. An ancient Decepticon leader known as "the Fallen" seeks revenge on Earth by activating a Star Harvester device hidden within the Egyptian pyramids—a machine capable of destroying the sun. The Autobots ultimately prevail when Optimus Prime absorbs the power of fallen Primes to destroy the machine and defeat the Fallen. The film starred LaBeouf alongside Megan Fox, Josh Duhamel, Tyrese Gibson, John Turturro, and Ramon Rodriguez, with Peter Cullen reprising the voice of Optimus Prime and Hugo Weaving as Megatron.

### Production

The film was produced on a budget of approximately $200 million, with an additional estimated $150 million spent on marketing—among the largest combined investments in Hollywood at the time. Production was notably impacted by the 2007–2008 Writers Guild of America strike: the script was developed under significant time pressure as Bay and writing team Roberto Orci, Alex Kurtzman, and Ehren Kruger worked with an incomplete screenplay. Principal photography spanned multiple continents, including locations in Egypt (Giza), Jordan (Petra), Pennsylvania, and California. The film made extensive use of IMAX cameras for key sequences—the first major Hollywood production to do so after *The Dark Knight* (2008)—resulting in approximately 25 minutes of expanded-ratio IMAX footage.

## Significance

*Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen* grossed $836.5 million worldwide ($402.1 million domestically, $434.2 million internationally), becoming the fourth-highest-grossing film of 2009 behind [*Avatar*]({{ '/news/arts-culture/avatar-2009/' | relative_url }}) ($2.79B), *Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince* ($934M), and *Ice Age: Dawn of the Dinosaurs* ($886M). The film earned a 19% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes based on 243 reviews; Roger Ebert called it "a horrible experience of unbearable length," and it won three Golden Raspberry Awards including Worst Picture, Worst Director (Bay), and Worst Screenplay. Conversely, audiences awarded it a "B+" rating on CinemaScore, illustrating the gulf between critical and commercial reception.

### The Twins Controversy

The film drew sustained controversy for two Autobot characters—Mudflap and Skids—whose designs, speech patterns, and behavior drew criticism from journalists and civil rights commentators as invoking African American stereotypes. Ebony magazine, among others, published pointed critiques, and the Associated Press covered the debate nationally. Bay initially denied the characters were intended as racial caricatures but pledged to reduce their screen presence in subsequent films; they did not appear in *Transformers: Dark of the Moon* (2011). The episode contributed to broader industry discussions about racial representation in high-budget filmmaking.

### Summer 2009 Box-Office Context

The film's June 24 opening landed amid one of the most competitive summer box-office seasons in years. [*Star Trek*]({{ '/news/arts-culture/star-trek-2009-film/' | relative_url }}) had opened May 8 to $385.7M worldwide; [*Up*]({{ '/news/arts-culture/up-pixar-2009/' | relative_url }}) arrived May 29 to critical acclaim; and [*Inglourious Basterds*]({{ '/news/arts-culture/inglourious-basterds-2009/' | relative_url }}) would open August 21. *Revenge of the Fallen* set an opening-weekend record for June at the time with $108.9 million domestically, before [*Avatar*]({{ '/news/arts-culture/avatar-2009/' | relative_url }}) reset box-office expectations entirely in December.

### Franchise Legacy

The film's commercial success—despite critical failure—demonstrated that the Transformers franchise could sustain a sequel cycle independent of critical reception, a lesson that reoriented studio thinking about critic-proof tentpole IP during the 2010s. Three further Transformers films followed under Bay's direction (*Dark of the Moon*, 2011; *Age of Extinction*, 2014; *The Last Knight*, 2017) before the franchise was partially rebooted with the well-received *Bumblebee* (2018).
